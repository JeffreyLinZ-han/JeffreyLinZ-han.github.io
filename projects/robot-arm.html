01 / System

Own the
whole chain.

W-D1 started as a way to learn what happens between a CAD model and a robot that can repeat a motion without excuses.

The interesting part wasn't any one component. It was getting mechanics, transmission, power, firmware, sensing, kinematics, and simulation to stop disagreeing with each other.

The shoulder and elbow use stepper-driven belt reductions, the wrist uses servo actuation, and an STM32 handles low-level commands from a Python interface. A MuJoCo twin mirrors the same joint structure for kinematics and trajectory work.

04 / Iteration

Most of the
project is the
parts that lost.

The final robot only shows the winning geometry. The table shows the real design process.

Joint housings changed. Link geometry changed. Base geometry changed. Parts were reprinted because clearances were wrong, loads were higher than expected, or a wire simply needed somewhere better to go.

I kept the failed and replaced parts because they are a better record of the engineering than a perfectly clean final render.
