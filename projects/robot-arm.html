W-D1 robotic arm

A six-axis desktop arm with STM32 control, absolute joint sensing, and a MuJoCo model.

Interactive twin → Back to homepage

I built W-D1 as an independent six-axis desktop arm, working through the mechanical design, electronics, STM32 firmware, and Python control interface. SolidWorks assemblies provided the joint layout for both the printed prototype and its MuJoCo model.

The mechanical work included timing-belt reductions, bearing and shaft supports, actuator mounts, and custom joint housings. I printed and assembled successive revisions to address belt tension, backlash, stiffness, part clearance, and cable routing. The photos below show the assembled arm, its transmission, and earlier printed parts.

For control, I integrated stepper and servo actuators with a NUCLEO-G474RE board and a serial Python GUI. The current firmware uses joint-encoder feedback to drive J2 and J3 toward their requested angles. Encoder freshness checks, joint limits, and a no-progress watchdog are included in that control path.

One debugging issue was an apparent angle reversal near the encoder zero crossing. The position register used a signed-magnitude value; treating it as an unsigned value and wrapping it at 4096 discarded the sign. I corrected the decoding and exposed both raw and signed readings in the telemetry so the command-to-measurement mapping could be checked directly.

The physical prototype runs demonstration motions, while the MuJoCo version can execute handwriting paths. The next work is to validate the revised encoder control on hardware and improve how simulated trajectories map to measured joint motion. The video here shows the real arm; the interactive twin exposes its joint motion in the browser.
